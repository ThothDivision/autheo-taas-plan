The story in one paragraph.

For anyone new to the project.

Banks, funds and issuers want to put bonds, fund shares, deposits and physical goods on blockchains, but those assets come with rules: who may own them, where, how much and when. Autheo's tokenization service (TaaS) issues such assets with the rules built in, and the DEX is where they trade. Before anything moves, the DEX checks every rule and explains the answer in plain words; when a trade happens, the asset and the payment swap in a single transaction, straight between the two parties' own wallets, or nothing happens at all. The same rules are enforced again on the blockchain itself, so no shortcut exists. A router can send a trade to outside exchanges and other chains when that is cheaper, and AI agents can shop and prepare trades for people within limits those people set, but only the person's own signature ever moves value. Every decision is written into a tamper-evident log.

Who it is for

  • Issuers and transfer agents who tokenize assets through TaaS and need their rules kept after issuance.
  • Eligible investors and institutions who want to buy, sell and move those assets across chains from their own wallets.
  • AI agents acting for people, under mandates, with receipts.
  • Outside venues (exchanges, AMMs, bridges) that can plug in as routes.
